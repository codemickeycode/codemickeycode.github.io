# codemickeycode.com — Personal Website Design Brief

**Working Concept:** The Industrial Third Space  
**Design Direction:** Clean Editorial × Cozy Industrial Café × Personal Field Journal  
**Status:** Design exploration, pre-mockup

## 1. Vision

**A thoughtfully designed third space where people can settle in, reflect, think deeply, and find inspiration.**

Create a personal website that feels like a third space on the internet — somewhere visitors can feel comfortable, explore ideas, encounter new perspectives, and spend time without feeling rushed.

Inspired by the atmosphere of independent coffee shops, industrial-style cafés, and bookstores: warm ambient lighting, exposed brick walls, natural textures, thoughtful interiors, and comfortable spaces that encourage people to stay.

The website should feel personal, welcoming, and intellectually stimulating without sacrificing clarity and professionalism.

### Core Philosophy

> Clarity invites people in. Warmth makes them want to stay. Curiosity gives them something to explore.

**Intentional in design. Purposeful in content. Unhurried in experience.**

The website should feel designed, but not manufactured. Personal, but not cluttered. Professional, but not corporate.

### Purpose

The website serves three complementary purposes:

**Personal Expression**

A home on the internet that reflects Mickey's identity, interests, values, experiences, and evolving ideas.

**Human Connection**

A place where friends, acquaintances, collaborators, and people she meets through conferences and events can get to know her beyond professional titles and find ways to stay connected.

**Visitor Experience**

A space that encourages visitors to slow down, reflect, explore questions, think deeply, and discover something meaningful or inspiring.

The goal is not necessarily to provide answers or inspire everyone. It is to create an environment where meaningful encounters with people and ideas can happen.

## 3. Primary Audience & Visitor Intent

The website is primarily intended for:

1. Friends and people who already know Mickey
2. People she meets at PyCon, Python community gatherings, conferences, and other events
3. Potential employers and professional collaborators

The website should help visitors get to know Mickey beyond her professional titles, understand what she does, discover her interests and ideas, and find meaningful ways to connect.

They may be interested in:

- Personal updates and reflections
- Travel, food, coffee, snorkeling, and books
- Local events, discoveries, and recommendations
- Current interests and experiments
- Projects, communities, and meaningful experiences

### 3.2 People Met Through Conferences and Events

**Visitor intent:** Learn more about Mickey after meeting her, understand her interests and work, and continue the conversation.

They may be interested in:

- Professional and technical background
- Projects and initiatives
- Python and open source contributions
- Community involvement and speaking engagements
- Learning experiments and current curiosities
- Shared interests beyond technology
- Ways to reconnect or collaborate

**Design implication:** Provide a clear introduction and straightforward access to projects, writing, and contact information.

The website should also serve as a conversation starter.

### 3.3 Potential Employers and Professional Collaborators

**Visitor intent:** Understand Mickey's capabilities, experience, accomplishments, and potential fit for opportunities.

They may be interested in:

- Professional background and career progression
- Technical and program management capabilities
- Selected projects and measurable outcomes
- Leadership and community-building experience
- Speaking engagements and publications
- CV, LinkedIn, and contact details

These audiences are not mutually exclusive. Someone Mickey meets at PyCon could become a friend, collaborator, or potential employer.

The website should therefore provide one coherent experience rather than separate journeys for different audiences.

**Personal first, professional without being corporate.**

Introduce Mickey as a whole person, not just through job titles and accomplishments.

**Easy to scan, rewarding to explore.**

Visitors should quickly understand who Mickey is and what she does, while having opportunities to explore more deeply.

**A conversation starter.**

**Accessible professional credibility.**

Employers and collaborators should not have to search extensively for relevant experience or evidence of capabilities.

- **Instagram:** Personal moments, photography, experiences, and everyday discoveries

## 4. Core Design Qualities

The website should consistently embody five qualities.

### 4.1 Warm

Create an atmosphere that feels welcoming, comfortable, and human.

Warmth should come from thoughtful use of color, typography, photography, textures, and writing.

### 4.2 Intentional

Every design decision should have a reason.

Typography, color, layout, whitespace, imagery, and interactions should contribute to clarity, comfort, or meaning.

Avoid adding elements simply because they look attractive or are fashionable.

**Guiding question:** Why is this element here?

### 4.3 Thoughtful

Encourage consideration rather than immediate reaction.

Present ideas with care, allow room for nuance, and communicate in a grounded, authentic voice.

The website should reflect deliberate thinking without feeling overly serious or academic.

### 4.4 Curious

Celebrate exploration, learning, questioning, and discovery.

Make space for unfinished ideas, experiments, changing perspectives, and connections between different interests.

Curiosity should be evident in both content and navigation.

### 4.5 Purposeful

The website should exist for more than self-presentation.

Its content should contribute to understanding, learning, connection, reflection, or inspiration.

Professional accomplishments and personal experiences should be shared with context and meaning rather than simply displayed.

**Guiding question:** What value might someone take away from this?

These qualities should inform the website's visual design, content strategy, interactions, and future development.

## 5. Core Visitor Experiences

The website should be designed around four experiences.

### 5.1 Settle In — Comfort

Create an environment where visitors feel welcome and comfortable spending time.

**Design implications:**
- Warm, inviting colors
- Readable typography
- Natural textures
- Generous whitespace
- Uncluttered layouts
- Simple, intuitive navigation

The website should feel like finding a comfortable corner in a favorite coffee shop.

### 5.2 Reflect — Stillness

Give visitors the space to slow down, consider ideas, and reflect on their own experiences.

**Design implications:**
- Calm visual pacing
- Thoughtful writing and personal observations
- Minimal distractions
- Comfortable reading widths
- Intentional pauses between sections
- Limited animation and visual noise

The design should not constantly demand attention.

### 5.3 Think Deeply — Intellectual Curiosity

Encourage deeper engagement with questions, ideas, systems, and human experiences.

**Design implications:**
- Essays, reflections, and field notes
- Questions worth exploring
- Projects that reveal the thinking behind decisions
- Reading lists and learning experiments
- Connections between ideas and disciplines
- Content that invites further exploration

The website should reward curiosity rather than encourage passive scrolling.

### 5.4 Find Inspiration — Discovery

Create opportunities for visitors to encounter ideas, experiences, and perspectives that might resonate with them.

**Design implications:**
- Personal stories and meaningful experiences
- Lessons from projects and experiments
- Books and ideas worth sharing
- Community initiatives and their human outcomes
- Curated photography and observations
- Discoveries from local makers, cafés, fairs, and cultural experiences
- Paths for visitors to discover related content

Inspiration should emerge naturally from authentic content rather than motivational messaging.

Discovery does not need to be extraordinary. Sometimes an interesting book, a thoughtfully made local product, a conversation over coffee, or an unfamiliar place can spark curiosity.

## 6. Supporting Design Principles

### 6.1 Clean, but Not Sterile

Use strong visual hierarchy, consistent alignment, and generous whitespace.

The website should be easy to navigate and comfortable to read, with enough character to avoid looking like a generic portfolio template.

### 6.2 Warm and Inviting

Take inspiration from cozy coffee shops and industrial interiors.

Create warmth through colors, typography, natural materials, subtle textures, and photography rather than excessive decoration.

### 6.3 Thoughtfully Personal

Use personal photographs, handwritten annotations, observations, and reflections to communicate personality.

Avoid exaggerated self-promotion and unnecessary corporate language.

### 6.4 A Living Space

Make room for unfinished ideas, questions, experiments, discoveries, and changing perspectives.

The website should evolve alongside Mickey's interests, experiences, and understanding of the world.

### 6.5 Substance Over Spectacle

Let projects, writing, photography, and meaningful experiences carry the design.

Avoid visual effects that compete with the content.

### 6.6 Intentional Simplicity

Simplicity should be a deliberate design choice rather than the absence of ideas.

Each component should contribute to the experience. Remove elements that introduce unnecessary complexity or distract from the intended purpose.

The website should feel effortless to visitors, even though the experience has been carefully considered.

## 7. Visual Identity

### Design Language

**Editorial Minimalism with Industrial Warmth**

Combine:

- The structure and precision of modern editorial design
- The warmth and tactility of industrial-style cafés
- The personality of a handwritten field journal
- The openness and organic movement of the ocean
- The craftsmanship and individuality associated with independent makers and local creative spaces

### Color Palette

| Color | Hex | Application |
|---|---|---|
| Warm Paper | `#F6F1E8` | Primary background |
| Espresso | `#3B302B` | Main typography and dark elements |
| Brick Terracotta | `#A65F49` | Warm industrial accents |
| Ocean Teal | `#4E7C82` | Links, buttons, and interactive elements |
| Warm Oak | `#C6A98C` | Supporting accents and subtle backgrounds |
| Sea Glass | `#D6EAE5` | Soft highlights and secondary backgrounds |
| Ube Plum | `#7B5D7A` | Optional occasional accent |

**Color Usage:**

- 70–80% warm neutrals
- 15–20% dark typography and structural elements
- 5–10% carefully chosen accent colors

Colors should be restrained. Not every color needs to appear on every page.

Color choices should support mood, visual hierarchy, and readability rather than decoration alone.

### Typography

Use three complementary styles.

**Editorial Serif — Headings**
- Possible font: Instrument Serif
- Expressive, elegant, and slightly literary
- Used for page titles and major section headings

**Clean Sans-Serif — Body**
- Possible fonts: DM Sans or Inter
- Readable, modern, and unobtrusive
- Used for paragraphs, navigation, buttons, and project information

**Handwriting — Personal Accents**
- Possible fonts: Caveat or Kalam
- Used for the signature tagline, short reflections, margin notes, and occasional annotations
- Consider incorporating actual handwriting for a more personal identity

Handwriting should feel intentional rather than decorative.

**Guideline:** At most one prominent handwritten element per major section.

## 8. Visual Elements and Textures

### Industrial Influences

Take inspiration from brick walls, warm lighting, wood, metal frames, and exposed architectural details.

Translate these into web design through:
- Clean grids and subtle borders
- Fine architectural lines
- Occasional brick-inspired textures
- Warm backgrounds
- Simple, deliberate compositions

Avoid literal café-themed illustrations or full-page brick-wall backgrounds.

### Organic Shapes

Use a very limited set of recurring motifs:
- Soft pebble-like shapes inspired by nature
- Thin curved lines inspired by ocean currents
- Occasional rounded or arched photographic frames

Shapes should support composition rather than compete with content.

### Photography

Prioritize original photography from Instagram and personal archives.

Recurring visual subjects include:
- Coffee cups and café interiors
- Food, desserts, and everyday discoveries
- Books, notebooks, and keyboards
- Snorkeling, marine life, and ocean views
- Travel destinations
- Friends, family, and community moments
- Coffee festivals, trade fairs, and independent markets
- Local brands, handmade products, interesting packaging, and craftsmanship

Photography should feel candid, observational, and authentic.

Use fewer photographs with more breathing room rather than recreating a dense Instagram grid.

Every photograph should contribute to a story, establish atmosphere, or provide insight into a meaningful experience.

### Local Maker and Craft Influences

Independent makers and local brands can provide inspiration through:
- Thoughtful product presentation
- Natural and tactile materials
- Distinctive but restrained packaging
- Handmade details
- Small-batch craftsmanship
- Stories behind products and creative processes

These influences should be incorporated selectively.

The website should not resemble an online marketplace or trade fair. Instead, it should borrow the sense of discovery, individuality, and appreciation for thoughtfully made things.

## 11. Homepage Structure

The homepage should provide a clear introduction while inviting further exploration.

### 1. Hero / Introduction

- Name and concise personal introduction
- Signature tagline: **Code. Passion. Freedom. Making a Difference.**
- Brief context about professional work and personal interests
- One visual anchor, such as a personal photograph or minimal organic shape
- Clear paths to learn more about Mickey and her work

The hero should help a visitor quickly answer:
- Who is Mickey?
- What does she do?
- What does she care about?

It should establish personality and atmosphere without overwhelming visitors.

### 2. Selected Work

Highlight two or three meaningful projects or initiatives.

Emphasize:
- Why the work matters
- Mickey's role and contributions
- The approach and decisions involved
- Outcomes, impact, and lessons

Provide a path to additional professional experience and accomplishments.

### 3. Current Curiosities

A small section featuring current questions, experiments, books, or ideas being explored.

This section should invite visitors into Mickey's thinking process and can evolve over time.

### 4. Outside the Code

A personal space for coffee, food, snorkeling, books, travel, local culture, and memorable experiences.

Use curated photography and short observations rather than a dense image gallery.

Consider featuring one or two recent discoveries from cafés, local makers, fairs, or travels.

The purpose is to share things that genuinely captured Mickey's interest, not to produce lifestyle content for its own sake.

### 5. Notes and Writing

Selected articles, essays, reflections, and field notes.

Prioritize writing that encourages reflection, deeper thinking, and discovery.

Provide an accessible path to the complete writing archive.

### 6. Stay Connected

A simple, low-pressure closing section inviting visitors to get in touch.

Include relevant contact methods and professional or social links.

Avoid aggressive calls to action or marketing-style messaging.

**Homepage priority:** Visitors should quickly understand who Mickey is, what she does, and what interests her. Those who want to explore further should find thoughtful content and clear paths to additional information.

Each section should have a clear purpose and contribute to the overall visitor experience.

## 12. Interaction and Movement

Movement should feel gentle and natural.

Consider:
- Subtle hover transitions
- Minimal fade-in effects
- Soft underline animations
- Occasional flowing lines

Avoid:
- Excessive parallax
- Continuous background animations
- Large animated blobs
- Distracting scroll effects
- Unnecessary motion that slows the experience

The site should remain responsive, accessible, and comfortable to browse on mobile devices.

Animation should never interfere with reading, reflection, or exploration.

Interactions should serve a purpose: clarify an action, improve navigation, or support discovery.

## 13. Design Constraints

### Do

- Maintain generous whitespace
- Use a consistent editorial layout
- Keep navigation simple
- Let typography and photography lead
- Use personal handwriting sparingly
- Apply industrial textures subtly
- Prioritize reading comfort and accessibility
- Make intentional design choices
- Ensure content has a clear purpose
- Create opportunities for reflection and discovery
- Celebrate authentic interests and meaningful experiences
- Keep professional information easy to find
- Allow the design to evolve alongside personal interests

### Avoid

- Corporate portfolio templates
- Overly polished personal branding
- Dense card-based layouts
- Excessive overlapping shapes
- Heavy gradients
- Overly saturated colors
- Decorative elements without purpose
- A literal coffee shop theme
- A marketplace-style presentation of local discoveries
- Constant calls to action
- Unnecessary complexity
- Hiding professional experience behind excessive exploration
- A homepage that attempts to show everything at once

## 14. Design Success Criteria

The website should successfully communicate:

1. **Professional Credibility:** Visitors can quickly understand what Mickey does, her capabilities, and what she has built.
2. **Personal Authenticity:** The design reflects her interests, values, and personality.
3. **Comfort:** Visitors feel welcome and can settle into the experience.
4. **Reflection:** The layout and content provide room for contemplation.
5. **Intellectual Curiosity:** Visitors encounter questions, ideas, and perspectives worth exploring.
6. **Inspiration:** The website creates opportunities for meaningful discoveries.
7. **Human Connection:** Visitors find reasons and opportunities to reconnect, collaborate, or start conversations.
8. **Intentionality:** Design elements have clear reasons for being there.
9. **Purposefulness:** Content contributes to understanding, learning, connection, or inspiration.
10. **Distinctiveness:** The website has a recognizable visual identity without excessive styling.
11. **Usability:** Both casual and professional visitors can quickly find relevant information.
12. **Discovery:** Visitors can explore personal interests, local culture, creative work, and experiences without feeling overwhelmed.
13. **Longevity:** The design can accommodate evolving interests and professional directions.

## 15. Design Decision Framework

Before introducing a new feature, visual element, or content section, consider:

**Intentionality**
- Why does this element exist?
- Does it improve the visitor experience?
- Does it contribute to clarity, comfort, or discovery?

**Purposefulness**
- What value does it provide?
- Does it encourage reflection, learning, connection, or inspiration?
- Does it reflect something meaningful about the person or the work?

**Audience Relevance**
- Who might benefit from this content?
- Can visitors find what they are looking for without unnecessary effort?
- Does it support meaningful connection or understanding?

**Simplicity**
- Can the same purpose be achieved with less?
- Does it introduce unnecessary visual or cognitive complexity?
- Would removing it improve the overall experience?

**Authenticity**
- Does it genuinely reflect Mickey's interests, values, or experiences?
- Does it communicate naturally rather than feel manufactured?
- Is the content being shared because it is genuinely interesting or meaningful?

Not every element must accomplish everything. But each should have a clear reason to exist.
